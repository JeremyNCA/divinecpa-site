New Tax Break: Deduct Your Car Loan Interest

3 min read

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act just introduced a valuable new deduction that could save you thousands. If you've financed a vehicle recently—or are planning to—you might be eligible to deduct up to $10,000 in car loan interest annually through 2028.

Key Requirements

  • Income limits: Phases out starting at $100,000 MAGI ($200,000 joint); eliminated at $150,000 ($250,000 joint)
  • Vehicle requirements: Must be under 14,000 lbs and assembled in the United States
  • Eligible vehicles: Passenger cars, minivans, vans, SUVs, pickup trucks, or motorcycles
  • Loan requirements: First-lien secured loan for personal use, originated after 2024

Refinancing is permitted under specific conditions, and lenders will file information returns showing your annual interest paid—making it easy to claim this deduction on your tax return.

Turn Business Losses Into Tax Savings With NOLs

4 min read

Had a tough year? Net Operating Loss (NOL) deductions can help your business weather the storm by averaging income across multiple years. This powerful tax strategy can ease the financial pain when revenue takes a hit.

What Qualifies as NOL?

  • Business operations that generate a loss
  • Casualty or theft losses from federally declared disasters
  • Rental property income losses

Important limitations: NOL deductions cannot offset more than 80% of your taxable income, with excess amounts carrying forward indefinitely. The excess business loss limitation (now permanent as of 2025) caps noncorporate business losses at $313,000 individually or $626,000 for joint filers plus other income annually.

If you have multiple NOLs, they apply in chronological order against modified taxable income—strategic planning can maximize your benefit.

Remote Work Tax Traps Every Employer Should Know

4 min read

Remote work isn't just changing where we work—it's creating a complex web of state tax obligations that can catch both employers and employees off guard.

For employees: You may face taxation in multiple states based on domicile or residency status. Typically, spending 183+ days in a state can trigger residency and tax obligations—even if you're just working remotely for an out-of-state employer.

Employer Tax Nexus Alert

  • Having remote employees can establish "nexus" in multiple states
  • May trigger income, payroll, franchise, gross receipts, and sales tax obligations
  • Compliance requirements vary significantly by state

Home office deductions: Unfortunately, employees generally cannot claim home office deductions under current tax law. However, employers can reimburse business expenses through accountable plans, making reimbursements both deductible for the company and excludable from employee income—a win-win arrangement.

Quick Tax Tips

Tax Strategies
1

Simplify Travel Expenses With Per Diem Rates

Skip the receipt hassle! The high-low method provides simplified employee reimbursement rates: $225 standard and $319 for high-cost locations (October 2025–September 2026). Employees only need to document time, place, and business purpose—no receipts required. Reimbursements are tax-free and not subject to payroll withholding. Note: Cannot be used for 10%+ business owners.

2

Year-End Strategy: Accelerate Your Deductions

With expanded state and local tax deductions, your itemized deductions might exceed the standard deduction. Consider accelerating qualified medical expenses (those exceeding 7.5% of AGI), home mortgage interest (up to $750,000 debt), and charitable contributions into 2025 to maximize your tax benefit this year.

3

Holiday Gifts: Know the Tax Consequences

Planning employee gifts? Achievement awards and de minimis gifts (like holiday turkeys or hams) can be deductible to employers and tax-free to employees when requirements are met. However, year-end cash bonuses are always taxable compensation—plan accordingly for payroll tax obligations.

Master Bill Tracking in QuickBooks Online

3 min read

Never miss a vendor payment again. QuickBooks Online provides powerful bill tracking through the Expenses section, helping you maintain great vendor relationships while staying on top of cash flow.

Essential Reports for Bill Management

  • Accounts Payable Aging Summary - See what's due and when
  • Bill Payment List - Track all payments made
  • Unpaid Bills - Never miss a due date
  • Vendor Balance Detail - Complete vendor payment history

The Cash Flow Dashboard and Statement of Cash Flows reports help you track obligations, while the Profit and Loss report shows how vendor expenses impact profitability.

Pro tip: QuickBooks Bill Pay Basic (included with most subscriptions) accepts invoice uploads and creates pre-filled bills automatically. You can pay vendors via ACH or paper check—streamlining your entire AP process.
