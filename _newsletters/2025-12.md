    icon: car
    read_time: 3
    content: >-
      The One Big Beautiful Bill Act just introduced a valuable new deduction
      that could save you thousands. If you've financed a vehicle recently—or
      are planning to—you might be eligible to deduct up to **$10,000 in car
      loan interest annually** through 2028.


      The deduction phases out starting at $100,000 MAGI ($200,000 for joint filers) and completely disappears at $150,000 ($250,000 joint).


      Qualifying vehicles must be passenger cars, minivans, vans, SUVs, pickup trucks, or motorcycles under 14,000 pounds gross vehicle weight rating. The vehicle must be new with final assembly in the United States.


      Loans must be first-lien secured for personal use, originated after 2024, and refinancing is permitted under specific conditions. Lenders must file information returns showing annual interest paid.
    takeaways:

  - title: Turn Business Losses Into Tax Savings With NOLs
    icon: money
    read_time: 4
    content: >-
      Had a tough year? Net Operating Loss (NOL) deductions can help your
      business weather the storm by averaging income across multiple years. This
      powerful tax strategy can ease the financial pain when revenue takes a
      hit.


      Qualifying losses stem from business operations, casualty/theft losses from federally declared disasters, or rental property income.


      **Important limitations:** NOL deductions cannot offset more than 80% of your taxable income, with excess amounts carrying forward indefinitely. The excess business loss limitation (now permanent as of 2025) caps noncorporate business losses at **$313,000 individually** or **$626,000 for joint filers** plus other income annually.


      If you have multiple NOLs, they apply in chronological order against modified taxable income—strategic planning can maximize your benefit.
    takeaways:
      - Business operations that generate a loss
      - Casualty or theft losses from federally declared disasters
      - Rental property income losses
  - title: Remote Work Tax Traps Every Employer Should Know
    icon: home
    read_time: 4
    content: >-
      Remote work isn't just changing where we work—it's creating a complex web
      of state tax obligations that can catch both employers and employees off
      guard.


      **For employees:** You may face taxation in multiple states based on domicile or residency status. Typically, spending 183+ days in a state can trigger residency and tax obligations—even if you're just working remotely for an out-of-state employer.


      **For employers:** Having remote employees can establish "nexus" in multiple states, triggering income, payroll, franchise, gross receipts, and sales tax obligations. Compliance requirements vary significantly by state.


      **Home office deductions:** Unfortunately, employees generally cannot claim home office deductions under current tax law. However, employers can reimburse business expenses through accountable plans, making reimbursements both deductible for the company and excludable from employee income—a win-win arrangement.
    takeaways:
      - Having remote employees can establish "nexus" in multiple states
      - May trigger income, payroll, franchise, gross receipts, and sales tax obligations
      - Compliance requirements vary significantly by state
